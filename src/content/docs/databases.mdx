Add a database from **+ Service → Databases**. It lands on the canvas as a staged change. Apply your staged changes and StackBlaze creates it with the catalog defaults. It joins the environment's private network.

To give a service the connection details, open the database, go to **Settings → Connection**, and switch on the services that should use it. Each connected service gets the database's connection [variables](/security/env-vars/). If a service connects to two databases of the same type, the second one's variables get a prefix (`<NAME>_`) so they do not clash.

Memcached, Kafka, RabbitMQ, ProxySQL, and object storage are under **+ Service → Add-ons** instead.

## Standard vs high availability

Every database starts as a single instance, except CockroachDB, which always runs at least three nodes. For some engines you can switch to high availability later.

<Diagram file="standard-vs-ha.svg" alt="Standard deployment is a single database pod with one volume. High-availability deployment is a primary plus replicas spread across nodes, fronted by a service, with streaming replication and automatic failover." />

*Standard runs one instance and one volume. HA runs a primary plus replicas across nodes, with automatic failover.*

### Standard

A single instance with its own disk. Simplest and cheapest. Fine for development, internal tools, and workloads that can take a short restart.

### High availability (HA)

Open the database and go to **Settings → HA**, then click **Enable HA**. HA is available on plans that include database high availability (Pro and Business). It is one-way: you cannot turn it off in place. To go back to one instance, restore a backup into a new database.

| Engine | What Enable HA does |
| --- | --- |
| PostgreSQL | Grows the cluster to 3 instances with streaming replication |
| MariaDB | Creates a new 3-node Galera cluster from a backup, moves your services to it, and keeps the old instance for rollback |
| Valkey | Adds a replica (master / replica) |
| FerretDB | Grows its PostgreSQL backend to 3 instances |
| OpenSearch | Grows the cluster to 3 nodes |
| RabbitMQ | Grows the cluster to 3 nodes |
| Kafka | Grows the cluster to 3 brokers |
| ClickHouse | Not available in place. HA (2 replicas plus ClickHouse Keeper) is chosen when the database is created |

Other engines have no HA switch.

:::note[Not every engine on every platform]
A platform admin can turn individual engines on or off, and some engines need a higher plan. The official MySQL and Redis images, OpenSearch, and ClickHouse are off until an admin turns them on. If an engine is not available on your plan, the catalog shows it with **Upgrade** instead of an add button.
:::

:::note[Compatible engines]
Some names in the catalog are served by a compatible engine. **Valkey** speaks the Redis protocol. **MariaDB** speaks the MySQL protocol. **FerretDB** and **DocumentDB** speak the MongoDB wire protocol on top of PostgreSQL. Your existing drivers work. Where the official image is offered too (MySQL, Redis, MongoDB), it is its own catalog entry.
:::

## Plan limits

PostgreSQL, MariaDB, Valkey, and the official MySQL, Redis, and MongoDB images show what your plan gives them under **Settings → Plan limits**: connections, backup retention, and for Valkey memory. PostgreSQL, MariaDB, and Redis databases count toward your plan's included databases.

## Working with a database

- **Settings → Access** (PostgreSQL, MariaDB, MySQL, MongoDB, FerretDB, DocumentDB, Valkey, Redis, ClickHouse) shows the in-cluster host, user, password, connection URL, and an environment snippet. For PostgreSQL, MySQL/MariaDB, Redis, and MongoDB you can also turn on **Public access** for the IP addresses you allow.
- **Logs** and **Metrics** tabs work like a service's.
- The CLI connects through a private tunnel: `stackblaze connect postgres`, `stackblaze connect mysql`, or `stackblaze connect redis`. `stackblaze addons info <addon> --reveal` prints the connection details.
- [Backups & recovery](/databases/backups/) covers recovery points for the whole environment. [Point-in-time recovery](/databases/pitr/) is available for PostgreSQL.

Looking for messaging? [Kafka](/messaging/kafka/) and [RabbitMQ](/messaging/rabbitmq/) are documented under Messaging. Files and media go in [object storage](/storage/object-storage/).
