---
title: Two-Factor Authentication
description: Turn on two-factor authentication with an authenticator app or a passkey, keep recovery codes, and add SSH keys.
---

Two-factor authentication (2FA) adds a second step to every sign-in, on top of your password or sign-in provider. StackBlaze supports two second factors:

- **Authenticator app** — any TOTP app (Google Authenticator, Authy, 1Password, Bitwarden, and others) gives you a 6-digit code.
- **Passkeys** — Face ID, Touch ID, a security key, or your device.

2FA is a setting on your own account. It applies in every organization you belong to.

## Turn on 2FA

1. Open **Personal settings → Authentication**.
2. Under **Two-factor authentication**, click **Set up**.
3. Scan the QR code with your authenticator app, or copy the key and enter it by hand.
4. Enter the 6-digit code from the app.
5. Save the recovery codes that appear. They are shown once.

Turning 2FA on signs you out of every other session. The browser you used stays signed in.

To add passkeys, use **Manage** next to **Passkeys** on the same tab.

## Sign in with 2FA

After your password (or sign-in provider), enter the current code from your authenticator app, or use a passkey. If you lost your phone, choose **Lost your phone? Use a recovery code**.

## Recovery codes

You get 10 single-use recovery codes. Each looks like `abcde-fgh23` and works once in place of a 2FA code.

To replace them, click **Generate new recovery codes** on the **Authentication** tab and confirm with a code from your authenticator app. Your old codes stop working.

If you lose your device and your recovery codes, an organization admin can reset your second factor. Otherwise, contact support.

## Reset your authenticator app

Moving to a new phone? Click **Reset** under **Two-factor authentication**. You confirm with your current factor, then scan a new QR code.

## Require stronger sign-in for the organization

There is no organization switch that forces 2FA. To control how members sign in, require SSO for the organization. See [SSO](/enterprise/saml/).

## SSH keys

SSH keys let you open a shell on your running services. They are not used for API login.

Add a public key (`ssh-ed25519` or `ssh-rsa`) under **Personal settings → SSH keys**. Then connect with `stackblaze ssh`, or with plain `ssh` to the host and port shown under **Connect** on that tab (`ssh.stackblaze.app`, port `2222`). The SSH user is the service (`project/environment/service`). `scp`, `sftp`, `rsync`, and `-L` port forwards work too.

```bash title="terminal"
ssh-keygen -t ed25519 -C "me@example.com"
# Paste ~/.ssh/id_ed25519.pub in Personal settings → SSH keys
```

API automation uses an `sb_pat_` token from **Personal settings → API tokens**. Tokens created before the `sb_pat_` prefix (`kbr_pat_…`) keep working.
