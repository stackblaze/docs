Single sign-on lets members sign in with their work account through your identity provider (IdP). StackBlaze supports **SAML 2.0** and **OpenID Connect (OIDC)**, for example Okta, Microsoft Entra ID, or Google Workspace.

SSO is available on the **Business** plan and on Enterprise agreements. Organization admins set it up themselves in **Settings → SSO & provisioning**. On other plans the page is listed but locked.

## Set up a connection

1. Open **Settings → SSO & provisioning** and choose **Connect an identity provider**.
2. Pick SAML or OIDC.
3. **Register StackBlaze at your identity provider.** Create an app at your IdP and enter the values the page shows:
   - SAML: **ACS / Reply URL** and **SP Entity ID / Audience**
   - OIDC: **Redirect URI**, with scopes `openid email profile`
4. **Identity provider details.** Paste what your IdP gives you:
   - SAML: the **IdP metadata URL**, or the **Sign-on URL (entryPoint)**, **IdP issuer / entity ID**, and certificate
   - OIDC: the **Issuer URL**, **Client ID**, and client secret
5. Run the connection test. It checks the details without saving them or signing anyone in.
6. Enable the connection, then use **Test sign-in** to sign in through it once yourself.

## Login domains

Add your email domains under **Login domains**. Each domain is verified with a DNS TXT record: add the record at your DNS provider, then click **Verify**. Members with a verified email domain are sent to your IdP when they sign in.

## Just-in-time provisioning

People on your verified domains get an account in the organization the first time they sign in through your IdP. Choose their role under **Default role for new members** (member by default). Each new account takes a seat; when the plan's member limit is reached, the sign-in is refused.

There is no SCIM provisioning.

## Require SSO

Under **Enforcement**, turn on **Require SSO** to make members sign in through your IdP. Before you turn it on, sign in through the connection once yourself.

While SSO is required, the connection cannot be pointed at a different IdP (protocol or issuer). Certificate and secret rotation still work.

If the organization leaves a plan that includes SSO, the connection stops signing people in and enforcement stops applying, so password sign-in works again. Nobody is locked out.

## SSO and roles

SSO decides how members sign in. [Roles and teams](/security/team-permissions/) still decide what they can do.
