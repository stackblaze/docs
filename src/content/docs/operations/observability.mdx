Every service has a **Metrics** tab and a **Logs** tab. For deeper analysis, open the service in **Coroot**. To keep logs in your own tools, add a log drain.

## Metrics

The service's **Metrics** tab shows **Resources** (CPU and memory) and **HTTP** (request rate and response time) over the last 2 hours, 24 hours, or 7 days.

From the CLI, `stackblaze metrics` shows current CPU and memory; `stackblaze metrics --since 24h` shows history (up to 7 days).

## Coroot

Click **Open in Coroot** on the Metrics tab. StackBlaze mints a one-time handoff and opens Coroot in a new tab, scoped to your service.

<Diagram file="coroot-handoff.svg" alt="A service in the dashboard mints a one-time handoff that opens Coroot for metrics, logs, traces, and a service map." />

| Signal | Use it for |
| --- | --- |
| CPU and memory | Right-sizing and catching OOMs |
| TCP connections (eBPF) | Unexpected outbound traffic |
| Golden signals / incidents | Error rate and latency regressions after a deploy |
| Service map | Who talks to whom inside the environment |

## Logs

The **Logs** tab streams stdout/stderr of the running service, with a search box. It also links to the build logs and system events. Log history is kept for your plan's **log retention** (shown under **Settings → Logs**).

From the CLI:

```bash
stackblaze logs                      # recent lines
stackblaze logs -f                   # follow live
stackblaze logs --search -q error --minutes 60
```

Or `GET /api/logs/{pipeline}/{phase}/{app}/`. An empty live tail is not the same as "no logs": the instance may have restarted, so search history.

## Log drains

Forward logs to your own logging service under **Settings → Log drains**. Each drain picks apps, a minimum level, and a destination: an HTTP endpoint, syslog, Datadog, or Quickwit. Your plan must include log drains.

## Tracing

On plans with tracing (Business), turn on **Tracing** in a service's settings and pick its language. Java, Node.js, Python, and .NET are instrumented automatically on the next deploy. For other languages, your OpenTelemetry SDK gets `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_ENDPOINT`, `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_PROTOCOL`, and `OTEL_SERVICE_NAME`. Traces show in Coroot.

You can also send OpenTelemetry from your app to Datadog, Honeycomb, or Grafana Cloud yourself; that is independent of StackBlaze.

## Notifications

Send project events (deploys, failures) to **Slack**, **Discord**, or any **webhook** under **Settings → Notifications**. Use Coroot incidents for SLO-style alerts.

## After a bad deploy

Open Coroot from the service, then roll back from the **Deployment** tab. See [Rollbacks](/cicd/rollbacks/).
