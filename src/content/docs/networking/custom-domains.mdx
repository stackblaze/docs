**Public networking** is how a service reaches the internet. A service with **Public** access gets a default URL under `stackblaze.app` with HTTPS when it deploys. You can add your own hostnames on top. Their TLS certificates are issued after DNS verification.

<Diagram file="custom-domain-dns.svg" alt="Diagram of CNAME from custom domain to StackBlaze edge with automatic TLS" />

*Point your DNS at the service's StackBlaze URL; certificates are issued and renewed automatically.*

## The default URL

StackBlaze generates the default URL from the service name plus a short random suffix, for example `api-k3f9.stackblaze.app`. It shows in **Settings → Domains** ("HTTPS included") and at the top of the service sheet. Until the first deploy it reads "Default URL assigned on deploy".

A service with **Internal** or **Worker** access has no public URL.

## Adding a domain

1. Open the service → **Settings → Domains** and click **+ Custom Domain**.
2. Enter the hostname, for example `app.example.com` or `example.com`. A hostname another account has already verified is refused.
3. Step 2 shows the DNS records to create. Copy them into your DNS provider and click **Done**.

The domain is listed as "Routed once DNS is verified" until it is live. Click **Configure DNS** on it to see the records and their status again.

## DNS records

Each custom domain needs two records:

| Type | Name | Value | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `TXT` | `_stackblaze-verify.app.example.com` | `stackblaze-verify=<token>` | Proves you own the domain |
| `CNAME` | `app.example.com` | the service's StackBlaze URL | Sends traffic to the service |

The dialog shows the exact token and target. The CNAME target is assigned after the service's first deploy.

### Apex domains (root domain)

An apex domain such as `example.com` cannot hold a CNAME. Use your DNS provider's **ALIAS** / **ANAME** record or **CNAME flattening** (Cloudflare) pointing at the same target. StackBlaze accepts a flattened record.

## Verification

StackBlaze re-checks pending domains every few minutes. Click **Verify now** in the DNS dialog to check right away (at most once every 10 seconds). It looks the records up through your DNS and public resolvers, so a record counts as soon as either sees it.

A domain moves through these states:

1. **Waiting for DNS** — the TXT or CNAME record is not visible yet.
2. **DNS verified** — both records resolve; StackBlaze adds the hostname to its edge.
3. **Issuing the HTTPS certificate** — usually a few minutes.
4. **Live** — "Verified — HTTPS certificate issued."

You can check your records yourself:

```bash title="Terminal"
dig _stackblaze-verify.app.example.com TXT
dig app.example.com CNAME
```

## SSL certificates

After DNS verification, the StackBlaze edge issues a Let's Encrypt certificate for the hostname and renews it automatically. See [SSL / TLS](/networking/ssl/).

## How many domains

Custom domains with automatic TLS are included on every plan. A workspace can add at most **20 new hostnames per 24 hours**; removing a domain does not give the allowance back.

## Removing a domain

Click the **−** next to the domain. StackBlaze removes it from the edge, then from the service.

## Troubleshooting

### Verification is stuck

If the domain stays on "Waiting for DNS", check:

1. Both records exist with the exact values from the dialog (copy-paste, don't retype)
2. The TTL on any previous records has expired (lower the TTL before making changes)
3. For an apex domain, your provider's ALIAS / flattening record points at the CNAME target
4. No CAA record on the domain excludes Let's Encrypt

### CAA records

If you have CAA records on your domain, make sure Let's Encrypt is allowed:

```text title="Required CAA record"
Type:  CAA
Name:  @
Value: 0 issue "letsencrypt.org"
```
